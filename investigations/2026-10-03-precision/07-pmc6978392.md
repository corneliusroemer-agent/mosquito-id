# PMC6978392 — Park et al. 2020, "Classification and Morphological Analysis of Vector Mosquitoes using Deep Convolutional Neural Networks"

**Verdict: not usable for the Mosquito Alert dataset. Do not train on it.**
Fetched anyway (120 MB, ~3 min) to establish that on evidence rather than from the abstract.

Source: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6978392/> · PMID 31974419 · doi:10.1038/s41598-020-57875-1 · *Sci Rep* 10, 2069 (2020).
Authors: Junyoung Park, Dong In Kim, Byoungjo Choi. Article CC-BY 4.0; **the dataset carries no licence at all** (see below).

## What the paper contains

A DCNN baseline study on Korean vector mosquitoes. The dataset (~3,600 raw images, 8 species,
6 training classes) is deposited on **Google Drive**, linked from the first author's GitHub
repo `jypark1994/MosquitoDL` — which itself contains only code (23 KB, no licence file, no data).
The Drive link is a bare file ID, not a folder, so it needs `gdown` rather than a plain fetch.

Two Drive artefacts: the 120 MB train/val + test zip (fetched) and a 2.5 GB raw-image zip (not fetched).

## Why it is not usable

**1. There is no *Aedes aegypti*.** The eight species are *Ae. albopictus*, *Ae. vexans*,
*Anopheles sinensis*, *Cx. pipiens*, *Cx. tritaeniorhynchus*, *Ae. dorsalis*, *Ae. koreikus*,
*Cx. inatomii*. The paper is about Korean vectors; *Ae. aegypti* is absent from the whole collection.
This alone disqualifies it for the aegypti gap.

**2. The *Anopheles* label is finer than the paper admits, and unverifiable.** The paper's Table 1
says "*Anopheles* spp."; the delivered folders say `Anopheles sinensis`. Nothing in the paper
justifies that narrowing, and *A. sinensis* is the only Korean anopheline, so the authors may have
applied it after the fact. A genus-level claim sharpened to a species with no stated basis is exactly
the kind of label we should not trust without an independent check — and we have no voucher or
expert identifier recorded for these.

**3. Wrong image type — this is the decisive problem.** Every image is a **dead, dried, preserved
specimen photographed through a digital microscope** (Nahwoo Pixit FHD One) on a flat grey
background, lit from one side, subject centred and filling the frame. The paper is explicit that it
did this *because* no field-like dataset existed. Our own benchmark already showed a specialist model
at 89.7% on curated data collapsing to 13–32% on real phone photos. This dataset is the curated end
of that gap in its most extreme form: there is no background clutter, no perspective distortion, no
motion blur, no partial occlusion, no human framing, no scale variation. Training on it would teach a
model to key on nothing that survives contact with a phone photo, and would inflate our validation
numbers against our real test set.

**4. Effectively far fewer specimens than the image count suggests.** Filenames are
`{brightness}_{posture}_{specimen-abbrev}_{index}.JPG`; grouping by specimen index gives:

| split | class | images | distinct specimens | img/spec |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Test | Aedes albopictus | 500 | 66 | 7.6 |
| Test | Aedes vexans | 500 | 70 | 7.1 |
| Test | Anopheles sinensis | 500 | 67 | 7.5 |
| Test | Culex pipiens | 470 | 73 | 6.4 |
| Test | Culex tritaeniorhynchus | 495 | 38 | 13.0 |
| Test | Non vectors | 495 | 42 | 11.8 |
| TrainVal | Aedes albopictus | 500 | 200 | 2.5 |
| TrainVal | Aedes vexans | 491 | 279 | 1.8 |
| TrainVal | Anopheles sinensis | 493 | 294 | 1.7 |
| TrainVal | Culex pipiens | 500 | 367 | 1.4 |
| TrainVal | Culex tritaeniorhynchus | 495 | 173 | 2.9 |
| TrainVal | Non vectors | 501 | 139 | 3.6 |

5,940 images total, but the anopheline set is ~360 *specimens* photographed under varying brightness
and posture. The 97.19% headline accuracy is a same-specimen, different-lighting recognition task.
The authors do state that validation and test accuracy diverged sharply (≈100% vs 97%) and attribute
it to overfitting; the specimen-level duplication is the mechanism, and it means the reported number
does not transfer to unseen individuals, let alone to unseen photographic conditions.

**5. `Non vectors` is a mixed bucket.** It pools *Ae. dorsalis*, *Ae. koreikus* and *Cx. inatomii*
(165 images each) into one label. Useless as a class; recoverable by filename only, and not worth it.

**6. No licence.** The GitHub repo has no LICENSE file (`gh api .../license` → 404) and the Drive
deposit carries no terms. The article's CC-BY covers the article's own figures and text, not a
separately-hosted dataset. Unlicence is not a licence: absent an explicit grant we have no permission
to redistribute or train on it, which is disqualifying on its own.

**7. Resolution is low and inconsistent.** TrainVal images are 420×314, Test images are 224×224
centre crops (the pipeline's own preprocessing), from 2952×1944 originals. The Test split has already
had the crop applied, so it is the least reusable half.

## Verification notes

- Species inventory counted from the delivered file tree, not from the paper's table.
- The "*Anopheles* spp." → `Anopheles sinensis` discrepancy is a mismatch between the paper and the
  data it deposited.
- Dimensions read from JPEG SOF headers for all 5,940 files; the 420×314 / 224×224 split by
  TrainVal/Test is clean and complete.
- Could not check whether TrainVal and Test share physical specimens: the index field is a
  per-split counter (Test runs 1–199, TrainVal 1–369), so cross-split specimen identity is not
  recoverable from filenames. A shared-specimen leak cannot be excluded — the conservative reading is
  that it is present.

## Did fetching earn its cost

Marginally yes, and it is now settled rather than open. The abstract-level reading ("3,600 images,
8 species, Open Access, on GitHub") would have left the microscope-image problem undiscovered, and
that is the fact that decides the case. The 120 MB is spent; the 2.5 GB raw archive is not worth
touching, since the blocker is the capture method, not the pixel count.

Retained: `mosquitodl_trainval_test.zip` (120 MB) plus `manifest.csv` (5,940 rows, per-file
dimensions, byte sizes, archive SHA-256, and the `NON_EDISTRIBUTION`/unlicence status recorded
explicitly) in `investigations/2026-10-03-precision/07-pmc6978392/`. Manifest columns follow
`06-aegypti-data/manifest.csv` conventions.

## What going further would tell us

Nothing that changes the verdict, and the next step is not more of this. If the goal is *Anopheles*
specifically, the productive direction is occurrence records with **voucher-backed or expert-identified
anophelines plus their photographs** — the Sri Lanka illustrated key referenced as ref 42
(doi:10.1007/s13355-016-0455-y) is a candidate for a taxonomic key with images, and GBIF/iNaturalist
*Anopheles* records are candidates for occurrence-linked photos. Both still need the same two checks
we applied here: capture medium (phone vs specimen) and who made the identification.
